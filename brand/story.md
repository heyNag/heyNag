## Voice and comic timing

Use plain language, specific observations, and enough conversational energy to sound interested. Short asides and a wry pause can make a technical explanation feel personal. Let the surprising fact do some of the work; every sentence does not need a joke.

The comedy reference becomes a rhythm: confident setup, slight hesitation, self-aware reaction, warm recovery. Write original lines. Aim humor at a harmless situation or one's own overthinking, and keep a visitor's confusion easy to resolve.

Talkative can mean generous explanation with an optional deeper layer. Give the main point first, then invite the reader into the details. A label such as **Show the nerdy details** can reveal the actual reasoning and cue the glasses pose. The content appears immediately; the character adds tone while the reader decides how far to explore.

Suggested lines are editorial examples, not quotes from Nag or claims about a completed project:

- "Here's the part I got curious about."
- "This is where I got a little carried away with the details."
- "That was a theory. Here's what happened."

## One question through the scene

A short character sequence should stay with the same question or artifact from beginning to end. Let curiosity, thinking, aha, and sharing describe one thought. Keep the project destination stable so the visitor can follow it without watching the performance.

An aha pose represents an idea or a satisfying observation. It does not prove a project was completed, deployed, or successful. Use nearby text to state the actual status. A wry oops can acknowledge an experiment needing another attempt, but it should not invent a failure in Nag's history.

## A useful longer story

Explain the question and who experiences it, Nag's actual contribution, the constraints, and one decision that mattered. Show what was tried, what changed, and what remains unfinished. A real demonstration and specific observations carry more meaning than broad claims about quality.

Distinguish designed, implemented, tested, deployed, and released. Use measured outcomes when they exist. Use qualitative observations when they are concrete. Describe private work only through an appropriate sanitized account.

Future stories can include art, everyday tools, learning, and experiments beyond software. A current platform or project does not have to define the whole identity. Keep the author visible, the character recognizable, and the invitation open.
