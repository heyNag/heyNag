Final opinion on the current prototype

Keep the little moth.
Refine how it acts.

The foundation is worth carrying forward. Its tiny proportions, unequal antennae, cream face and layered green wings survived the review. The work is ready for a focused refinement pass; it has not earned blanket approval as a finished public brand.

Protect the innocence. Keep the oversized head, short mittens, attached stub feet, four marked wing panels and longer viewer-right antenna. Improve gestures through pose, space and timing. Do not grow the body, lengthen the arms, or add detail to every emotion.

This is an audit, not a new design. Production artwork, code and profile draft were held unchanged. The images above are existing assets.

Ordered refinement priorities

The changes that would earn their place.

01
Before a unified V5 GitHub rollout

Make the identity survive its actual size.

The V2 header is still active in the README. A small circular V5 avatar does not exist, and the legacy crop clips the long paddle. Full-body art loses details at 32–48px. Create a purpose-built V5 head crop and matching headers, preserving the unequal antenna cue.

On dark grounds, the current rig’s antenna stems fade while the green tips remain bright. Test a restrained local edge or background adjustment at 96–160px. A broad glow would change the material without necessarily repairing the thin connection.

Evidence: actual-size comparison · local README dark proof. This is a legibility finding, not a claim that SVG parts physically detach.

02
Before using affected transitions in a story

Preserve the feeling between feelings.

Sad → worried briefly opens a small green smile around 200–300ms, then returns to concern. Blending coordinates smoothly is not enough. Carry the appropriate mouth and gaze into the next action instead of passing through the smiling rest pose.

Keep the existing interruption mechanism; refine which facial and body channels carry the old intention. A neutral bridge remains useful when the story actually calls for recovery.

Evidence: deterministic transition samples. Exact source-engine poses, not frames from the exported reel.

03
Next animation refinement

Give each movement a reason and a rhythm.

The wings already vary: they open for excitement, narrow for fear, and stay quieter during thought. Keep that restraint. The concern is not that every emotion needs a new flap.

The shared easing stops movement at every keyframe; the excited hop has a mathematical stop during descent at 900ms. Compare purposeful through-motion with the present version at normal speed. Improve mitten separation within the existing short reach before considering new drawings. Prioritize curiosity, thought, happiness and the mischievous peek as signature performances.

Evidence: hop poses · thinking contact poses · all held wing postures. The perceptual size of the timing issue remains to be judged in continuous playback.

04
Before sharing the interactive studio publicly

Keep the viewer in control.

Keyboard activation of a late gallery card scrolls to the player but leaves focus far below it. Pressing Tab returns to the gallery, hides the player and pauses the performance. Align focus and viewport; provide a nearby way to select another emotion.

Preserve the good foundations: no initial autoplay, explicit play/pause, finite actions, expressive reduced-motion stills and visible focus. Optional future sound should have its own deliberate control.

Evidence: reproduced keyboard sequence · control checks. No full accessibility-conformance claim is made.

05
Personal-brand refinement

Let the character lead back to Nag’s work.

The name and first-person introduction already remain clear in the README. Strengthen this with one real decision, tradeoff or discovery from a project. The desired dry wit should come through original words and timing, not a borrowed sitcom mannerism.

Build one short “notice → investigate → share” sequence around a verified project fragment before expanding environments. The moth notices it, works something out, then invites the visitor to look. Test whether people remember Nag and that work as well as the creature.

No invented biography, project outcome or audience reaction is needed. Update stale stage instructions and declare which assets are current, prototype or historical.

Cross-examination

Agreement was not the acceptance test.

A finding was withdrawn. An initial interaction concern alleged that updating the static expression note removed a disclosure and link. A live DOM check showed that the reviewer had confused a class with a child element’s ID. The disclosure and links were intact; the finding was removed.

Next creative proof

A small story can test more than another sheet.

First, four clear beats

Notice a real project fragment. Lean in and think. Let the idea land. Turn back and share it. Keep the same object present throughout and make the scene understandable without a label or sound.

After that works, the meditation peek can become a quiet character scene. Weather can interrupt an action and resolve. Holidays can dress a familiar behavior. Each addition should reveal intention.

Then, ask unfamiliar people

Show a short application without explaining the desired personality. Ask what they remember, who it is about, and what they expect to find. Check recall of Nag and one project separately from liking the moth.

The audience reviewer proposes a small formative study, including delayed recall after 24–48 hours. It is a way to learn, not a prerequisite for every reversible profile update or a statistical proof of universal appeal.
