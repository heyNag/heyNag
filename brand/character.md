# The curious heyNag moth

The moth brings Nag's stated personality into the work: **fun, talkative, curious, and happy to get geeky**. It notices something, gets absorbed, and turns back to share the interesting part. Nag remains the person speaking and the author of the projects. The character needs no separate name or invented biography.

Its warmth comes from attention and conversation, carried by a tiny, innocent character. A small discovery can earn a big reaction; a difficult detail may later bring out the glasses and an amused grin. Comic timing and expressive range must fit that small body, without turning it into an adult presenter.

- **Unequal antennae:** the character's left antenna, viewer-right in a frontal drawing, is permanently longer and sits higher. The right antenna is shorter and reaches sideways. Both have broad rounded chartreuse paddle tips and clear separate stems. The asymmetry is anatomical, so preserve it through turns and avoid mirroring the artwork.
- **Innocent face:** a dominant broad cream head, soft cheek fullness, charcoal oval eyes, gentle brows, and a small friendly mouth. Preserve the head-to-body ratio, eye spacing, and receptive expression. Do not default to a knowing grin or mature facial attitude.
- **Tiny body and hands:** a small soft ruff above a tiny rounded cream torso. Exactly two short arms stay close to the body and end in little mitten hands. Preserve clear boundaries between hands, cheeks, ruff, and wing seams. Do not lengthen the arms or enlarge the hands to accommodate a future pose.
- **Layered moth wings:** each pale forewing overlaps a smaller, richer green hindwing. Distinct tips and restrained green markings make the wings legible as appendages. Keep the compact relaxed envelope, visible overlaps, and clear separation from the arms; do not return to the continuous scalloped v2 cape.
- **Stub feet and stable stance:** two short rounded chartreuse feet sit immediately below the body, with almost no exposed shin. Keep the small stable stance. No long human legs, adult hip/shoulder opposition, or taller torso for articulation.

Light and dark treatments must preserve the same proportions, anatomy, face, and wing markings. Restrained edge contrast may keep the outer contour and antenna stems readable on dark grounds. A night treatment is the same little character, without a new glowing identity. The [design system](design-system.md) owns color tokens; [the world guide](world.md) retains later setting ideas.

## Character behavior: notice, get into it, share

The long antenna notices first. The eyes and head follow after a small delay; the body catches up. The moth investigates, sometimes becoming delightfully absorbed. Then it looks back toward the visitor and opens a hand toward the discovery.

That final return is the emotional payoff: an invitation to join the thought. Antenna asymmetry gives this behavior a visible anchor in the avatar and the full-body character. Use the pattern around a real question, detail, or result; its meaning should remain clear when presented as a single still.

Future performances should read through silhouette and attention before captions or motion marks. Adapt the supported thinking pose to the tiny body rather than lengthening its limbs. Curiosity needs an object of attention; a round surprised mouth alone does not express it. Thinking stays playful and absorbed. Oops accompanies a harmless surprise; serious errors need clear information and a composed expression.

## Glasses belong to deep work

Glasses are a temporary cue explicitly chosen by Nag for the geeky, absorbed side of the character. Use softly rectangular charcoal frames with clear lenses. Place the frame perimeter outside the oval eyes, and retain room for brow and mouth acting. The shape contrasts with the rounded face without turning it stern.

Keep the everyday master and small identity avatar glasses-free. A detailed scene may carry the glasses through a transition into an idea; the bulb then becomes the single active prop. An independently animated glasses-on gesture needs two hands, a clear path past the antennae, and a rigged accessory. A static glasses pose does not imply that gesture has been built.

## Small and large uses

The V5 head avatar is a circular-safe composition of the existing vector head, with complete unequal antenna paddles, broad innocent face, and simple expression. Day and night PNG/SVG exports have actual circular-mask checks at 32, 48, 64, 96, 128, and 160px. Use at least 32px; detail loss is expected at the smallest size. The day avatar’s paper ground is the universal GitHub account option; an account avatar does not auto-switch themes. See [application provenance](source/applications-v5.json). The V2 avatar remains historical. The reviewed full-body artwork has a recommended minimum height of 96px; the proof includes 64px, 96px, and 160px comparisons.

Later, larger illustrations may let the little character gesture or investigate a restrained setting. Keep the same tiny proportions at every size. Props must serve a specific beat; [world.md](world.md) parks that visual vocabulary, and [story.md](story.md) keeps it tied to Nag's real work.
