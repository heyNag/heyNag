# The v5 expression library

This guide covers **24 static expressions in light and dark**, built on the tiny, innocent [v5 character](character.md). The library covers core emotions and useful cognitive and social states. It is a curated set of distinct performances, not a requirement to invent a new face for every synonym.

The 24 static drawings are available in paired light and dark treatments. Comic, form, and motion reviewers challenged their anatomy and acting; revisions cleared the thinking lenses, separated curious/happy/aha, softened Focused, and distinguished Surprise from Scared. The [acting catalogue](expressions/acting.json) describes the selected drawings and separate future motion notes; the [expression studio](expressions/index.html) displays the actual assets. These drawings remain local studies. Their separate [V5 motion studio](animation-v5/index.html) now provides articulated performances; neither library implies a public release.

## Construction rules

- Preserve the v5 big soft head, broad cheek fullness, tiny rounded torso, small ruff, little mitten hands, and stub feet. The same construction serves happy and difficult feelings. Do not make a taller, thinner, older, or more human character to obtain a pose.
- Keep exactly two short arms. Hands remain near the torso, ruff, lower cheek, or lower mouth. Bring the head down to meet a mitten when needed; do not lengthen an arm to reach the crown or high sides of the head. No articulated human fingers, long shins, adult hip/shoulder opposition, or body stretch.
- Thinking uses a compact folded support arm beneath the other elbow, with the upper mitten contacting the lower chin. Maintain clear boundaries between both hands, ruff, chin, and wing seams. The pose must remain physically plausible within the existing tiny body.
- Preserve the permanently longer character-left antenna, viewer-right in the frontal master, and the shorter opposite antenna. Their bends may express emotion; their lengths, attachment points, and identity do not swap. Keep separate stems and rounded paddle tips.
- Each pale forewing overlaps a smaller, richer green hindwing. Preserve those layers, restrained markings, distinct tips, and the separation between arms and wings. Change the wing opening or attitude without returning to a single cape shape.
- Use the existing charcoal oval eyes, gentle brows, and small mouth vocabulary. Temporary squints and asymmetric expressions are permitted. Permanent half-lidded superiority, sharp diagonal sneers, mature smirks, new noses, or extra facial features are not.
- Give each state one dominant action and at most one supporting cue or prop. Most states need none. Glasses count as the prop; do not add a symbol to a glasses state. No scene furniture, backgrounds, weather, holiday objects, or decorative bursts.
- Thinking and Focused share **softly rectangular charcoal glasses with rounded corners and clear lenses**. Retain the oval eyes and room for brow acting. Lens interiors should read as clear lenses over the cream face, not oversized white eyeballs or opaque white patches.

## Differences that must survive a small render

| Closely related states | Distinct acting intention |
| --- | --- |
| Curious / Confused / Skeptical | Curious approaches with empty low hands; Confused retreats and tucks with small open mittens; Skeptical draws back to inspect, with an open eye and one questioning mitten. |
| Thinking / Focused / Aha | Thinking looks upward with real lower-chin support; Focused aims its gaze steadily toward a point with low quiet hands; Aha lifts the head, antenna, and one wing without stretching the body. |
| Happy / Excited / Proud | Happy rests comfortably; Excited becomes buoyant; Proud settles upright with a modest satisfied smile. A bigger smile alone is not the difference. |
| Surprised / Scared / Oops | Surprised expands upright with a round O-mouth and empty outward mittens; Scared tucks its chin toward both mittens and recoils; Oops dips toward one lower-mouth or lower-cheek mitten in a sheepish pause. |
| Grateful / Affectionate / Shy | Grateful acknowledges the viewer with hands at the chest; Affectionate offers warmth with hands below the ruff and one small heart; Shy turns its gaze down and sideways, with low tucked hands. |
| Sad / Worried / Scared | Sad sinks with empty hanging hands; Worried folds inward around clasped mittens; Scared recoils and protects the chest. |
| Angry / Frustrated / Disgusted | Angry protests outward; Frustrated contracts around being stuck; Disgusted pulls away and declines with one little outward mitten. |
| Mischievous / Sleepy | Mischievous quietly peeks with one softly half-open oval eye and an alert long antenna; Sleepy has two heavy eyes, a yawn, and relaxed antennae. |

Use 128–160px cells for the full library. The 96px view is a compact contextual check: neighboring feelings such as pride, gratitude and shyness benefit from labels or context. Visual review is not a blind recognition study. Tiny avatar use remains a separate composition problem.

## Four sheets of six

Each transparent sheet uses three columns and two rows, in the row-major order listed. Use a consistent head/body scale, generous separation, complete appendages, and no printed labels inside the artwork. Each still should communicate its dominant action without requiring animation or a caption.

### Anchors: curiosity, thought, and innocent play

| Cell | State | Readable acting intention | Antennae and wings | Supporting cue |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 0 | **Curious** | Open attentive eyes, one gently raised brow, tiny parted mouth. Lean the whole head toward an off-frame interest with empty mittens held low. | Long antenna reaches first; wings stay quietly back. | None. |
| 1 | **Thinking** | Eyes look upward through the glasses; thoughtful unequal brows and a small absorbed grin. One compact arm supports the opposite elbow; the other mitten meets the lower chin as the head settles toward it. One stub toe is poised to tap. | Antennae curve softly forward; wings fold close. | Glasses. |
| 2 | **Happy** | Open oval eyes, gently lifted brows, and a rounded open smile. Rest in a relaxed little stance with one mitten slightly open beside the belly. | Antennae relax; wings sit comfortably open. | None. |
| 3 | **Mischievous** | Pretend to sleep while peeking: one softly half-open oval eye, the other peacefully closed, and a tiny centered U-smile. Keep mittens tucked close and the body compact. | Long antenna remains alert despite the pretend sleep; wings fold quietly. | None. |
| 4 | **Aha** | Eyes brighten, brows rise, and the mouth makes a delighted rounded “oh.” Lift the head and one mitten beside the lower cheek; do not stretch the torso. | Long antenna springs up; one wing lifts first. | One small bulb, clear of the antennae. |
| 5 | **Oops** | Round eyes, an offset sheepish brow, and a small rounded mouth. Pause with one mitten at the lower mouth or lower cheek, bringing the head down to meet it; feet draw together. | Short antenna droops; long antenna hesitates halfway up; wings pull inward. | None. |

### Joy: positive and social feelings

| Cell | State | Readable acting intention | Antennae and wings | Supporting cue |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 0 | **Laughing** | Eyes become happy closed arcs; brows relax and the mouth opens in a broad rounded laugh. Tip the head back slightly, with mittens against the belly. | Antennae swing outward; wings spread loosely. | None. |
| 1 | **Excited** | Very open eyes, high brows, and an open U-shaped smile. Both short mittens lift beside the ruff; both stub feet barely leave the baseline. Keep the body its original size. | Antennae spring apart; wings flare together. | None. |
| 2 | **Proud** | Settled open eyes and a small satisfied smile. Lift the chin modestly, with mittens near the belly and feet planted; no taller torso or adult pose. | Antennae stand comfortably; wings open a little. | None. |
| 3 | **Grateful** | Soft eyes meet the viewer, inner brows lift gently, and the mouth makes a small closed smile. Touch both mittens to the upper chest and dip the head in acknowledgment. | Antennae incline forward; wings settle close. | None. |
| 4 | **Affectionate** | Warm curved eyes, soft brows, and a small rounded smile. Meet the mittens gently below the ruff and tilt the head toward the viewer. | Antennae curve outward gently; wings softly cup the body. | One small heart. |
| 5 | **Shy** | Glance down and sideways beneath lightly raised brows, with a small restrained smile. Turn the head slightly away; tuck mittens low near the belly and keep feet together. | Antennae lean inward; wings close protectively. | None. |

### Mind: understanding, uncertainty, and energy

| Cell | State | Readable acting intention | Antennae and wings | Supporting cue |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 0 | **Confused** | Unequal eye openings, inward brows, and a tiny sideways “o.” Retreat and tuck the head while both mittens turn upward close to the belly. | Antennae bend in different directions; wings sit unevenly. | None. |
| 1 | **Focused** | Through the same glasses as Thinking, aim steady eyes toward one nearby point. Lower both brows slightly and use a tiny straight mouth. Lean the head down a fraction; keep mittens quiet and low. | Antennae aim toward the same point; wings fold neatly. | Glasses. |
| 2 | **Skeptical** | Keep both eyes open, one brow raised and the other level, with a small flat mouth. Draw the head back to inspect; turn one little mitten outward in a questioning gesture. No sneer. | Long antenna bends back; wings remain still and closed. | None. |
| 3 | **Surprised** | Round eyes, both brows high, and a large round O-mouth. Rise upright with both empty mittens spreading away from the body. | Antennae spring upright; wings open evenly. | None. |
| 4 | **Relieved** | Close the eyes gently, release the brows, and make a tiny exhaling mouth. Settle the head; rest one mitten on the chest while the other loosens outward. | Antennae relax outward; wings lower. | At most one small breath curl, if needed. |
| 5 | **Sleepy** | Let both eyes droop heavily beneath soft brows and a small rounded yawn. One mitten covers the lower mouth as the head slumps toward a shoulder. | Both antennae sag softly; wings fold slackly. | None. |

### Tender: difficult feelings

| Cell | State | Readable acting intention | Antennae and wings | Supporting cue |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 0 | **Sad** | Lower the gaze, lift the inner brows, and turn the small mouth down. Let the head and empty mittens hang, with feet close together. | Antennae droop; wings lower without wrapping the body. | None. |
| 1 | **Worried** | Look sideways with pinched raised inner brows and a small uneven closed mouth. Clasp the mittens at the belly and hunch the head inward. | Antennae curve toward each other; wings fold tightly. | None. |
| 2 | **Scared** | Open the eyes wide beneath high pinched brows; use a narrow open mouth. Recoil, tucking the chin toward both mittens at the ruff, with stub feet braced together. | Antennae sweep backward; wings draw forward protectively. | None. |
| 3 | **Frustrated** | Squeeze the eyes shut beneath uneven pinched brows, with a small strained mouth. Huddle both mittens below the ruff and press or tap one stub toe. No hands at the high sides of the head. | Antennae bend into opposing curves; wings droop unevenly. | None. |
| 4 | **Angry** | Keep open eyes beneath lowered inward brows and a small emphatic frown. Plant the feet and hold tiny clenched mittens at the sides; the face remains round and innocent. | Antennae stiffen outward; wings flare slightly in protest. | None. |
| 5 | **Disgusted** | Partly squeeze the eyes shut, pinch the brows, and make a small wavy grimace. Recoil and turn one mitten outward to decline; keep the other near the belly. | Antennae pull away from the subject; wings retract. | None. |

## Light and dark are matching performances

The night version is the **same pose and drawing** as its day counterpart: identical face, hand contacts, head/body ratio, antenna bends, wing layers, markings, crop, scale, and cell order. Use the v5 warmer cream and restrained fine olive edge light to maintain readability on the dark ground. Do not regenerate a different emotion, add night props, turn the character luminous, or alter the linework to make a second design.

Check actual transparency on light and dark backgrounds. A checkerboard, white matte, or dark baked-in field is not transparency. Keep the antennae, wing tips, and feet inside every cell's safe area. Compare the day/night pairs before calling them matched.

## Stage boundary

**Now:** static expression sheets and their matching light/dark treatments. Review identity, tiny proportions, distinct acting, hand contacts, transparency, and scale using the actual rendered proof.

**Current companion implementation:** the [V5 motion studio](animation-v5/index.html) articulates the face, glasses, antennae, four wings and little limbs while preserving the tiny model. It provides finite playback, reduced-motion stills and inspection controls. The original raster drawings remain the visual reference; the vector rig is an editable interpretation. See [motion.md](motion.md) for construction and verification limits.

**Later:** scenes, weather, and holidays. Those settings must not be introduced into this expression stage. The [world guide](world.md) retains that later vocabulary; [motion.md](motion.md) records existing studies and their limits.
