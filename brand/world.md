# A small world around the work

**These setting ideas are parked.** Current work is the v5 character and its light/dark treatment. Expressions and motion follow, then scenes, weather, and holidays. Keep the existing scene experiments as history; do not expand them during character review.

The future world is a lightly drawn place for noticing, thinking, and showing things. It may contain a note, a cord, a useful fragment, or a small idea bulb. Nag's projects and words supply the subject without requiring mascot lore.

## A shared visual language

Use the favored v5 character's soft head, tiny round body, little mittens, stub feet, charcoal contour, warm cream, and layered green wings. Later props must fit that small innocent character; do not enlarge its hands or lengthen its limbs. Draw props with blunt ends and simple geometry, with outlines quieter than the face. A future stage may use paper or ink from [the design system](design-system.md).

One composition has **one primary prop** and, when helpful, two or three small motion marks. Keep clear space around the eyes, hands, antennae, and the actual project content. A pose should remain understandable when the marks are removed. Avoid decorative objects that merely announce a profession or dress the character for an unrelated theme.

Glasses are a temporary character accessory for deep work. They can remain on during the transition to an idea, but the bulb becomes the single active prop. Keep the rest of that scene clear.

## The useful objects

| Element | Purpose | Drawing and behavior |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Blank note** | A detail worth looking at or a place worth returning to. | One cream rectangle with a soft corner; blank unless it contains legible, truthful content. A slight turn can follow the character's inspection. |
| **Connecting cord** | An attempt to connect two things. | One short, gently curved cord with simple ends. Keep it clear of the character's limbs and antennae. It illustrates a question; the accompanying story explains the actual integration. |
| **Glasses** | Nag's absorbed, geeky mode. | Clear softly rectangular lenses, charcoal frames, eyes fully visible. No lens glare that hides the expression. |
| **Idea bulb** | The instant a thought clicks. | A small rounded bulb above and slightly beside the head, clear of the long antenna. One short warm glow and a few restrained rays; the still bulb works with motion off. |
| **Real project fragment** | Something worth sharing. | An actual frame, interface detail, authored diagram, or other verified artifact. Keep it accurate and legible. The moth presents it without covering the useful part. |

A stylized blank note is an illustration. A project screenshot is evidence. Label or position them so the visitor can tell what they are seeing. Do not place invented product UI inside a character scene to suggest a demonstration exists.

## Parked scene ideas

- **Arrival:** an open stage and a compact hand wave near the face. The welcoming hand becomes an open conversational gesture after the wave; the wings stay folded.
- **Something caught my eye:** a note or cord end sits just within reach. The long antenna notices it first; the body leans in and makes a second look. Uneven antennae and an open hand can express uncertainty without a question-mark cloud.
- **Getting into the details:** the glasses are on, one forearm supports the other elbow, and the hand rests under the chin. The relaxed grin and brief foot tap keep deep work lively. Clear space lets the silhouette carry the thought.
- **There it is:** the head lifts, the wings open to the broadest shape in the set, and a small bulb glows above the head. Allow a short pause before the realization; the return glance invites the visitor into it.
- **Look at this:** one real fragment sits beside the character. The moth glances from it to the visitor, presents it with a directional hand, and shifts aside so a normal project link stays easy to find.
- **Well, that happened:** the same object stays in place while the moth pauses, offers a wry shrug, and resets. The humor belongs to the harmless situation, never to a visitor's mistake.

## Day and night

The current [character proof](character-proof/index.html) shows the locally reviewed v5 day/night candidates. They preserve the same innocent anatomy and remain local drafts pending final approval. No setting, prop, or scene is required to approve the character. The following stage ideas are retained for later.

Day may use a paper ground and enough space for the charcoal outline to breathe. A restrained cream or chartreuse field may establish the character's immediate area.

Night may use an ink ground with a restrained pool of light or pale stage. Keep antenna stems, tiny hands, and layered wing contours distinct. Preserve the same anatomy, face, and markings; lighting must not create a different glowing creature. These are optional future settings, not requirements for the current light/dark export.

Both settings support the same gestures and content. Night can feel absorbed and cozy; day can feel open and conversational. These are art-direction options for future scenes, not a requirement for a landscape, sky, furniture, ambient particles, or a constantly glowing interface.

## Use and production status

This is a parked setting guide. The v2 lab retains static poses, finite whole-body transforms, and a small idea cue. The separate v2-derived articulated study includes a seated tree vignette. Neither is the current v5 model. [motion.md](motion.md) records the study boundaries and deferred requirements.

Keep the amount of world proportional to the task. A GitHub header may need only the character, name, and one readable gesture. A longer project story can earn a scene because there is a real question to investigate. Every setting should return attention to Nag and the work.
